# Example: Tuesday Pool Care

Tuesday Pool Care is a made-up, one-truck pool service in Phoenix. The owner wants a short video for customers whose pool turned green after a summer storm. This folder holds every file the three skills made for that one video, so you can see what yours will look like. The other files were written to match these answers and show what each skill produces.

In a real My Videos folder, `voice.txt` sits at the top and the rest sit in a folder named `2026-08-12 green pool after a storm`. Here they share one folder so they're easy to browse.

## The files, in the order they were made

1. **answers.txt** (made by capture-story). The owner's spoken answers to the four questions, saved exactly as they came out of dictation, "um" and all. Nothing here is cleaned up, because this file is the sample of how the owner really talks.
2. **brief.txt** (capture-story). The plan for the video: the topic, who it's for, the opening line and the real story quoted word for word, what to do after watching, the order, and the words the owner never says. It was saved only after the owner said "yes, those are my words and my order."
3. **voice.txt** (capture-story). Short notes on how the owner talks, each with a quote as proof, plus the full answers. It keeps the newest five videos' answers, so it grows with each video.
4. **script-first-draft.txt** (draft-script). The first script, written after the owner approved the outline. At the time it was saved as script.txt. It's kept here under a different name so you can compare. Look closely and you'll find four lines the owner never said.
5. **check.txt** (check-script, run in a new chat). The report on that first draft. It found the four lines: a promise the owner never made (a free checkup), an "it's not this, it's that" line, a teasing "the best part?", and a list of three that sounds like a brochure.
6. **script.txt** (draft-script again). The script the owner recorded, 169 words in five takes.

## What the owner decided

The check suggests and the owner decides. For each finding:

1. Free checkup: not true. The owner used their own close instead: "just call me, or call your pool guy, whoever."
2. "It's not your chemicals. It's your timer.": cut. The opening line already says "you probably didn't do anything wrong," so the owner swapped in their own words from answers.txt: "two, three days later it's green."
3. "The best part?": took the suggestion, "That's it. Takes five minutes."
4. The list of three: took the suggestion, the owner's own sentence from answers.txt with the middle trimmed.

Then the owner went back to the chat that wrote the script and said "change these lines," naming each one. Every other line stayed as it was.

## Why the first draft had made-up lines

draft-script is told to invent nothing. Drafts still slip, and the chat that wrote a script tends to read right past its own habits. That's why the check runs in a fresh chat, and why you read the script out loud before you record.
